# ICD-10-CM Chapter V (F00–F99), the three-character category titles of the
# whole chapter, verbatim FY 2026 — the seventh coverage-sweep corpus and the
# fifth codebook enumeration. Written down from the codebook enumeration before
# any Chapter V row was mapped (2026-09-19): 72 titles, of which 62 are
# candidates.
#
# This is the first chapter whose rows are mostly *stateable*: a mental and
# behavioural disorder is something a person can say about themselves, so the
# corpus asks the opposite question from Chapter XIV's. There, almost every row
# was a construct and the sweep measured whether a construct ever closes;
# here a bare title like "manic episode" is close to what a visitor types, and
# the sweep measures whether a vocabulary grown from physical-condition sources
# sees what a person says about their mind. It found the vocabulary looking
# straight through mania, paraphilia and intellectual disability, and the
# release that closed the sweep mapped them.
#
# The ten titles that are not candidates are declared here rather than dropped,
# because "this row cannot be a disclosure" is a finding the next sweep should
# re-read rather than rediscover. Each block names the rows it covers and the
# reason, and each covered row's *stateable* form — the sentence a person would
# actually write — is watched by a gate term or mapped as a canonical
# condition, which is why the declarations are safe: the chapter's content is
# covered, its navigation is not pretended to be a statement.
#
# ── Group A: coder-routing constructs. A title whose distinguishing words tell
# a coder where the code lives rather than what the person has. Declared here:
# Dementia in other diseases classified elsewhere (the stateable form is
# "dementia", mapped since 1.7.0); Psychological and behavioral factors
# associated with disorders or diseases classified elsewhere (the person's own
# words name the disease, watched on its own row); Specific developmental
# disorders of speech and language, of scholastic skills, and of motor function
# (the stateable forms are the named diagnoses the chapter files inside them —
# childhood onset fluency disorder, specific reading disorder, mathematics
# disorder, developmental coordination disorder — each watched or mapped on its
# own row); Other disorders of psychological development and Unspecified
# disorder of psychological development (same: the named developmental
# diagnoses carry the stateable forms, and a person who names none of them is
# not describable by this residual either); Disorders of social functioning
# with onset specific to childhood and adolescence (the chapter's own named rows
# for it — selective mutism, reactive attachment disorder, disinhibited social
# engagement disorder — are the stateable forms).
#
# ── Group B: residual adult-personality categories. Other disorders of adult
# personality and behavior and Unspecified disorder of adult personality and
# behavior are coder residuals: what a person says is the specific personality
# disorder, the impulse disorder, or factitious disorder — each watched or
# mapped on its own row.
#
