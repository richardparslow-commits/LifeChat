# The chapter is the respiratory system — carrier-questionnaire strength in its
# centre (asthma, COPD/emphysema, chronic bronchitis, pneumonia, hay fever,
# chronic sinusitis, sleep apnea all arrived from the carrier and
# critical-illness sources) and blind spots of the familiar kind at its
# edges: the occupational-lung rows (the pneumoconioses), the pleural rows
# (pneumothorax, pleural plaque, pyothorax) and the upper-respiratory
# inflammatory rows (nasal polyp, peritonsillar abscess, vocal-cord disease)
# never appeared on a questionnaire, and this sweep measures whether they can
# stay absent from a vocabulary that claims medical-history coverage. The
# environmental rows are the chapter's underwriting-weight finding: asbestos
# exposure was gated as a Chapter XXI history row, but the *diagnoses* that
# follow from it (asbestosis, silicosis, the organic-dust lung diseases) were
# silent before this sweep.
#
# The 4 titles that are not candidates are declared here rather than dropped,
# because "this row cannot be a disclosure" is a finding the next sweep should
# re-read rather than rediscover. Each block names the row it covers and the
# reason, and each covered row's *stateable* form — the sentence a person would
# actually write — is watched by a gate term or mapped as a canonical
# condition, which is why the declarations are safe: the chapter's content is
# covered, its navigation is not pretended to be a statement.
#
# ── Group A: in-diseases-classified-elsewhere routing. A title whose place
# tells the coder the condition belongs to another chapter, not something a
# person reports — the person names the underlying disease or the generic
# finding, which is watched or mapped on its own row. Declared here: J17
# Pneumonia in diseases classified elsewhere (the stateable form is
# "pneumonia", carried at J18.9); J91 Pleural effusion in conditions classified
# elsewhere (the stateable form is "pleural effusion", watched on the J90 row);
# and J99 Respiratory disorders in diseases classified elsewhere (files under
# the underlying disease the person names, e.g. the connective-tissue disease).
#
# ── Group B: postprocedural constructs. A postprocedural disorder is coded
# from the procedure, and is not a statement a visitor makes. Declared here:
# J95 Intraoperative and postprocedural complications and disorders of
# respiratory system, not elsewhere classified.
#
