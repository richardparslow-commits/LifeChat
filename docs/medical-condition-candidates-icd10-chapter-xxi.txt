# ICD-10-CM Chapter XXI (Z00–Z99), the three-character category titles of the
# whole chapter, verbatim FY 2026 — the sixth coverage-sweep corpus and the
# fourth codebook enumeration. Written down before any of its rows were mapped
# (2026-09-19): 90 titles, of which 42 are candidates.
#
# The 48 titles that are not candidates are declared here rather than dropped,
# because "this row cannot be a disclosure" is a finding the next sweep should
# re-read rather than rediscover. Each block names the rows it covers and the
# reason, and each row's *stateable* form — the sentence a person would actually
# write — is watched by a gate term or mapped as a canonical condition, which is
# why the declarations are safe: the chapter's content is covered, its
# navigation is not pretended to be a statement.
#
# ── Group A: encounter constructs. An encounter code records why a visit
# happened, not something the person reports, so no visitor sentence contains
# one. Declared here: Encounter for general examination without complaint,
# suspected or reported diagnosis; Encounter for other special examination
# without complaint, suspected or reported diagnosis; Encounter for
# administrative examination; Encounter for examination and observation for
# other reasons; Encounter for immunization; Immunization not carried out and
# underimmunization status; Encounter for other prophylactic measures; Encounter
# for contraceptive management; Encounter for procreative management; Encounter
# for antenatal screening of mother; Outcome of delivery; Liveborn infants
# according to place of birth and type of delivery; Encounter for maternal
# postpartum care and examination; Encounter for prophylactic surgery; Encounter
# for procedures for purposes other than remedying health state; Encounter for
# plastic and reconstructive surgery following medical procedure or healed
# injury; Encounter for fitting and adjustment of other devices; Orthopedic
# aftercare; Encounter for other postprocedural aftercare; Encounter for other
# aftercare and medical care; Persons encountering health services for specific
# procedures and health care, not carried out; Encounter for mental health
# services for victim and perpetrator of abuse (the disclosure itself — "I was
# abused", "childhood abuse" — is gated by its own terms); Counseling related to
# sexual attitude, behavior and orientation; Persons encountering health
# services for other counseling and medical advice, not elsewhere classified.
#
# ── Group B: socioeconomic and psychosocial circumstances. A circumstance the
# codebook files as a problem — unemployment, housing, schooling, life
# management — is not a health condition and is not this product's subject
# matter either, so gating the words would only catch the product's own copy.
# Declared here: Problems related to education and literacy; Problems related to
# employment and unemployment; Occupational exposure to risk factors (the
# substances a person names — asbestos — are gated instead); Problems related to
# physical environment; Problems related to housing and economic circumstances;
# Problems related to social environment; Problems related to upbringing; Other
# problems related to primary support group, including family circumstances;
# Problems related to certain psychosocial circumstances; Problems related to
# other psychosocial circumstances; Problems related to lifestyle (each
# lifestyle fact is watched by its own field rule); Problems related to life
# management difficulty; Problems related to care provider dependency; Problems
# related to medical facilities and other health care; Persons encountering
# health services in other circumstances.
#
# ── Group C: report and risk-factor constructs. A resistance pattern belongs to
# an organism, a receptor status to a pathology report, and "other specified
# health status" to a coder with more information than a visitor has. Declared
# here: Resistance to antimicrobial drugs; Estrogen, and other hormones and
# factors receptor status; Other specified health status; Other contact with and
# (suspected) exposures hazardous to health (the exposure words — asbestos,
# lead — are gated); Personal risk factors, not elsewhere classified.
#
# ── Group D: device, prosthesis and machine states. The stateable form is the
# thing a person names — "I am an amputee", "I have a prosthetic leg", "I am on
# a ventilator", "I have a colostomy" — and each of those is gated; the category
# wording ("Presence of other devices") is not a sentence anyone writes.
# Declared here: Acquired absence of limb; Presence of other devices; Other
# postprocedural states; Dependence on enabling machines and devices, not
# elsewhere classified.
